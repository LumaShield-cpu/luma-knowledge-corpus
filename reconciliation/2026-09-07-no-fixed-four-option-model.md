> Shield's law. This copy is kept here as a secondary reference for

**Date:** 2026-09-07
**Author:** Stacey (via reconciliation session)

## Conflict

[corpus/architecture/shield-anatomy.md](../corpus/architecture/shield-anatomy.md)
states a structural law drawn from the V3 chat export: "everything
works in fours" — a Shield has exactly 4 Pads around a core. (A related
but distinct claim was already cleared in
[2026-09-07-four-pads-vs-rejected-numerology.md](2026-09-07-four-pads-vs-rejected-numerology.md) —
that reconciliation concerned a rejected *packet-schema* numerology
claim, not this one.)

[shield-volume-ii-approval-incorporation-addendum-v0.2.1.md](../sources/docs/shield-volume-ii-approval-incorporation-addendum-v0.2.1.md)
— a formally approved, hash-bound, human-signed instrument (Stacey
Malitowski, 2026-07-17) — freezes, as Decision 5:

> Option order is a contextual, constraint-first partial order; **no
> universal attention score or fixed four-option model governs every
> Shield.**

This is a direct statement that the number of Pads/options is *not*
architecturally fixed at four for every Shield — which reads as
contradicting the "everything works in fours" structural law.

## Evidence

- Decision 5 is frozen, approved working architecture as of
  2026-07-17, with a recorded approval ID and signature.
- The "everything works in fours" claim traces to an undated,
  informal chat export (V3), which per this corpus's own
  source-authority conventions ranks as design history, not settled
  architecture.
- Decision 6 (same addendum, also frozen) draws a scope distinction:
  *"Packs, Stacks, and Paths are optional structures in universal
  SHIELD, while Packs and Stacks are essential candidate structures for
  future LumaShield."* This establishes that **universal SHIELD
  architecture and a specific product's (LumaShield's) design choices
  are different things** — universal SHIELD leaves structures optional;
  a specific product can still commit to a fixed structure for itself.

## Resolution

**The universal SHIELD architecture does not mandate a fixed
four-option/four-Pad model.** Decision 5 wins over the informal "four
is a structural law" framing — it is better-evidenced (approved,
signed, hash-bound) and explicitly, deliberately worded to rule out
exactly this kind of universal numerical law.

**This does not mean LumaShield's 4-Pad interface is wrong** — per
Decision 6's universal/product distinction, LumaShield specifically may
still choose 4 Pads as its own product-level design decision (and the
LumaPatentOS inventory and Shield Universe atlas both independently
describe LumaShield's actual interface as having 4 Pads). What's wrong
is treating "four" as an architectural *law* that governs every Shield
across the whole ecosystem — it's a LumaShield product choice, not a
SHIELD constitutional constraint.
